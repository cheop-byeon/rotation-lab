# Rotation Lab · speaker script

Seven slides, about 4 minutes. Read it as it is: short sentences, plain words. Pronunciation hints are in brackets at the top of a slide; don't read them out.

## 1. Cover

Hi, this is Rotation Lab, for the Field Shift challenge.

Rotation Lab is for farmers in Norway. You find your farm, you choose one of your fields, and you see its story.

It uses forty-five years of NASA weather, and every satellite picture of that field since 2020.

The farmer still makes the decision. We just bring the evidence.

## 2. The challenge

Here is the problem.

What you plant this year changes your soil, your pests and your harvests for the next few years.

At the same time, the weather is changing. Frost, dry spells and heat don’t come when they used to.

NASA has decades of data about this. But it’s spread out, and it’s hard to use it for one real field.

So our question is simple: for this one field, what grows well, when should I plant, and in what order?

## 3. Built on open data

Everything runs on open data, and most of it is NASA’s.

First, NASA POWER. It gives daily weather and soil moisture for the field, all the way back to 1981.

Second, NASA HLS. These are satellite pictures from Landsat and Sentinel-2. We use every clear picture of the field since 2020.

We also use long-term averages, to see if a month is normal or not. And NASA map layers, for context.

Norway adds two open records: the property map, which gives us the outline of the farm’s land, and the crops each farm reports every year.

The farmer adds what only they know, like drainage and their plans.

## 4. What the satellite saw, and what the farm reported

(Stjørdal: say “STYUR-dahl”.)

Let me show you one real field. It’s on a farm in Stjørdal, in Norway.

The farm reports its crops every year. It grew winter wheat, then barley in 2020, then grass from 2021.

Now look from space. In 2020 the field was bare in May, and then it stayed green into October. That’s barley with grass sown under it, to start next year’s grass.

The app’s guess from the satellite fits the farm’s own record in seven seasons out of seven.

And to keep it fair, we compare like with like: this year’s grass with earlier grass years on this field, never with a grain year.

## 5. What has changed around these fields

We also compare the 1990s with today.

In Stjørdal, the frost-free season is eighteen days longer. The last spring frost comes twelve days earlier. That’s good news for many crops.

But for potatoes, the warm, wet weather that brings potato blight went up from about nine days to thirteen days a season.

And farms are changing too. Barley went from forty-four to thirty-six percent of the farmland, while wheat doubled.

We only show a change when the whole record points the same way. And we don’t call it good or bad. That depends on the farmer’s crops.

## 6. Evidence and options, not orders

Why should you trust it?

We checked what the satellite sees against what farms reported to the government. On the example field, it fit in every season. Around the field, grass came within a few percent. These checks also helped us find and fix mistakes.

When the app is not sure, it says so, and it tells you why.

It never gives orders. What we check is the farmer’s own plan.

And every finding links to its evidence: the charts and the records behind it. So you can check it yourself.

## 7. Try it

So that’s Rotation Lab. Find your farm, and read your fields from space.

You can try it right now: rotation-lab-tawny dot vercel dot app. There’s an example farm in Stjørdal on the first page.

The code is open on GitHub.

Thank you! We’re happy to take your questions.
